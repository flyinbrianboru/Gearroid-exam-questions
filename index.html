How to answer each question in the oral

  1. Translate it — say the Irish clearly.
  2. Name the grammar — “Is é an saorbhriathar atá anseo.”
  3. Give another way to say it — a different word, the Ulster form, or change the person or tense.

Main forms are standard (An Caighdeán). Ulster forms are given as alternatives, because examiners like hearing them.

An ‘á’

Being done — the ‘á’ construction

Nótaí gramadaí · Grammar notes

á + verbal noun = something is being done. Pattern: Tá + [the thing] + á + [verbal noun] (+ ag + who is doing it)

Tá an obair á déanamh. — The work is being done.
Bhí an teach á phéinteáil ag Seán. — The house was being painted by Seán.

Á is really ag/do + a (“at its / her / their”). So it mutates like a (his / her / their), and the thing being done decides the mutation.

The three mutations

The thing is… á + … Examples
masculine singular séimhiú an teach á phéinteáil · an carr á dheisiú · an bia á ithe (vowel: no change)
feminine singular h before a vowel, no séimhiú deoch á hól · an obair á déanamh · an litir á scríobh
plural (or two things) urú rialacha á bhfoghlaim · na geataí á n-oscailt · airgead agus am á gcaitheamh

l, n, r, s, m can't take an urú, and l, n, r don't show a séimhiú: na leabhair á léamh · tá siad dár moladh.

To change the tense, change only bí
Tá an obair á déanamh. is being done
Bhí an obair á déanamh. was being done
Beidh an obair á déanamh. will be being done
Bíonn an obair á déanamh gach lá. is (usually) being done every day
Níl … / An bhfuil … ? / Nach bhfuil … ? negative / question

The full set — doing something to a person

The same construction for “they're looking for me / you / him…”. Verb: cuardach (Ulster: cuartú).

Object Form Mutation Example English
me do mo (do m' + vowel) séimhiú Tá siad do mo chuardach. They're looking for me.
you do do (do d' + vowel) séimhiú Tá siad do do chuardach. They're looking for you.
him / it (m) á séimhiú Tá siad á chuardach. They're looking for him.
her / it (f) á h + vowel Tá siad á cuardach. They're looking for her.
us dár urú Tá siad dár gcuardach. They're looking for us.
you (pl.) do bhur urú Tá siad do bhur gcuardach. They're looking for you all.
them á urú Tá siad á gcuardach. They're looking for them.

Also: Bhí mé do mo ghléasadh — I was getting dressed.

Don't mix these three up
Tá obair le déanamh. There's work to be done. (not started)
Tá an obair á déanamh. The work is being done. (in progress)
Tá an obair déanta. The work is done. (finished)

Ulster note: in older Donegal writing you may meet dhá / dá for this á (e.g. dhá dhéanamh). It's the same thing.

An Saorbhriathar Caite

Past autonomous — “was done”

Nótaí gramadaí · Grammar notes

The saorbhriathar says an action happened without saying who did it. English uses was / were + -ed.

Briseadh an fhuinneog. — The window was broken (somebody broke it).
Rugadh mé i mBéal Feirste. — I was born in Belfast.

Making it — regular verbs

Verb type Ending Examples
1st conjugation (1 syllable) -adh / -eadh bris → briseadh · dún → dúnadh · tóg → tógadh · ól → óladh · ith → itheadh · léigh → léadh · díol → díoladh · togh → toghadh
2nd conjugation (-igh, -ail, -ir…) -aíodh / -íodh bunaigh → bunaíodh · ceannaigh → ceannaíodh · oscail → osclaíodh · imir → imríodh · athraigh → athraíodh

Three golden rules

  1. No séimhiú, no d' — Briseadh (not Bhriseadh) · Óladh (not D'óladh) · Fágadh (not D'fhágadh).
  2. The object is é / í / iad, never sé / sí / siad — Briseadh é. Chonacthas í. Itheadh iad.
  3. Negative níor, question ar, still no séimhiú — Níor briseadh é. Ar briseadh é?

Gníomh nó staid? Action or state? (the exam trap)

Action → saorbhriathar State / result → bí + aidiacht bhriathartha
Briseadh é inné. — It was broken yesterday (someone broke it). Bhí sé briste nuair a fuair mé é. — It was (already) broken when I found it.
Bunaíodh an coláiste i 1849. — It was established (the event). Bhí an coláiste bunaithe le blianta. — It had been established for years.
Dúnadh an siopa ar a sé. — The shop was closed at six. Bhí an siopa dúnta. — The shop was closed (not open).

Quick test: can you add “by someone” or “at a time”? → action → saorbhriathar. Does it describe how something was? → bí + participle.

“Have done / had done” — participle + ag
Tá na leabhair léite againn. We have read the books.
An bhfuil an scannán feicthe agaibh? Have you (pl.) seen the film?
Bhí an obair déanta aige. He had done the work.

Participles: briste · léite · feicthe · ite · ólta · déanta · bunaithe · oscailte · dúnta · scríofa · ceannaithe · tógtha · foghlamtha · tagtha · imithe

Céimeanna Comparáide

Comparatives & superlatives

Nótaí gramadaí · Grammar notes

Making the comparative form

Most adjectives slenderise and add -e. Adjectives ending in -ach → -aí, and -úil → -úla:

ard → airde · óg → óige · sean → sine · láidir → láidre · deacair → deacra · fuar → fuaire · deas → deise · tábhachtach → tábhachtaí · dúshlánach → dúshlánaí · suimiúil → suimiúla · tapa → tapúla

Irregular — learn these off
maith → fearr olc → measa mór → mó beag → lú
furasta → fusa fada → faide te → teo minic → minice
breá → breátha álainn → áille gearr → giorra dócha → dóichí

Present Past

Relative clauses — who / which / that / whose / where

Nótaí gramadaí · Grammar notes

1. Direct relative — “who / which / that” is the subject or object

a + séimhiú (keep d' in the past: a d'ól, a d'fhoghlaim)
an fear a bhíonn ann the man who is (usually) there
an leabhar a léigh mé the book that I read
an rud a deir tú what you say (deir never takes séimhiú)
an mac léinn atá ag foghlaim the student who is learning (tá → atá)
gach rud a d'fhoghlaim mé everything I learned

2. Negative relative — “who / that … not”

nach + urú (present, future, irregular past) · nár + séimhiú (regular past)
céim nach bhfuil dúshlánach a degree that isn't challenging
na daoine nach ndearna an obair the people who didn't do the work
an leabhar nár léigh mé the book I didn't read

3. Indirect relative — “whose / where / to whom / in which”

Use it when the clause has a possessive (a mhac, a hathair) or a preposition (ann, air, di). a + urú (present, future, irregular past: a raibh, a bhfaca, a ndearna, a bhfuair, a ndeachaigh, a ndúirt) · ar + séimhiú (regular past)
an bhean a bhfuil a mac sa rang the woman whose son is in the class
an áit a bhfuil mé i mo chónaí the place where I live
an teach a raibh mé ann the house I was in
an bhean ar thug mé an leabhar di the woman I gave the book to
an baile ar rugadh mé ann the town I was born in

4. Emphasis (fronting) — Is é … a …

Is é Seán a bhris é. — It was Seán who broke it.
Is ar an gCoiste Téarmaíochta a thiteann an obair. — It's on the Terminology Committee the work falls.
Ní in aon lá amháin a tógadh an Róimh. — It wasn't in one day that Rome was built.

“If” sentences — real and hypothetical

Nótaí gramadaí · Grammar notes
Real / possible Hypothetical (“would”)

Rules to remember
